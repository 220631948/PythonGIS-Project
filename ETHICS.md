# Ethics & Privacy Policy 🛡️

The Cape Town GeoHub is committed to the responsible use of spatial data. We acknowledge that mapping urban environments, particularly regarding safety and informal services, has significant social implications.

## 1. Data Aggregation & Anonymization

### Safety & Crime Statistics
- **Precinct Level only:** Crime data is never displayed at the individual incident or address level. It is aggregated to the SAPS Precinct level to prevent the stigmatization of specific streets or households.
- **No Victim Data:** We do not store or display any information regarding victims, witnesses, or suspects.
- **Not Predictive:** This tool is intended for historical urban planning and resource allocation. It is explicitly **not** intended for predictive policing or real-time surveillance.

### Informal Settlements
- **Service Access Focus:** Data regarding informal settlements is used exclusively to highlight gaps in basic service delivery (water, sanitation, electricity).
- **Privacy First:** We use official boundary data provided by the City of Cape Town and do not include household-level mapping.

## 2. Transparency

- **Source Attribution:** Every data layer in the application includes a direct link to the source at the City of Cape Town Open Data Portal.
- **Methodology:** Our spatial analysis algorithms (e.g., Access Scoring) are open-source and documented in `backend/app/spatial_analysis/`.

## 3. Responsible Use

We encourage researchers and planners to use this tool to:
- Identify underserved areas for infrastructure investment.
- Analyze walking connectivity to public transport.
- Visualize urban density and land use patterns.

We strictly discourage using this tool for:
- Commercial exploitation of vulnerable communities.
- Property valuation based on biased crime metrics.
- Targeted surveillance.

## 4. Disclaimer

*The Cape Town GeoHub provides estimates based on available open data. While we strive for accuracy, the City of Cape Town's datasets are subject to update cycles and may not reflect real-time ground conditions. Users should consult official City departments for critical planning decisions.*
